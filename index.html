Project 1: Interactive Audio & Visual with p5.js

I was inspired by haunted history of the Chateau Marmont (a known haven for celebrities from the age of old hollywood to now), and of the obsession/voyeuristic nature of celebrity. This interactive scene is of the hallway of the Chateau Marmont hotel, following a glint of light, until it reverses its view from inside the room. I used an arrow and zoom transition inspired by the Google Maps streetview, which is supposed to allude to the voyeurism and the expectation of visibility from celebrities. As Google Maps streetview makes streets visibly accessible to the public, placing the arrow feature inside a confined space creates a tension between privacy and visibility that I found very interesting. The last image is of an eye looking through the peephole from inside of the room, back out into the hallway, signifying that there was actually no one trying to look inside the room, but rather, a psychological paranoia of the inhabitant the entire time. Playing on the themes of visibility and privacy, when visibility is constantly expected of oneself, even private moments can feel like a spectacle. Final: The Chateau

Project 2: HyperNarrative

Inspired by Martin Scorsese's surreal film After Hours (1985), in which a man in New York endures one mishap after another, entering a nightmarish series of events that prevent him from going home, I wanted to create an interactive storyline traversing through urban American Realist paintings by the artist, Edward Hopper, accompanied by quotes from Jean Paul Sartre's "Nausea" (1938). The story follows different characters in Hopper's paintings in their idle stares amidst their mundane realities, along with the existentialist, somewhat nihilistic words of Sartre. All their storylines lead to the most surreal of Hopper's works (and his last painting), "Two Comedians" (1966), with one of the most affirming and hopeful excerpt from "Nausea," bridging the dichotomies of isolation and collective conscious. Another detail to note is every time "beginning" is mentioned, it is hyperlinked to a randomized page, which correlates to Sartre's view of non-linear time; as he states, "all that was not present did not exist. The past did not exist. Not at all. Not in things, not even in my thoughts. It is true that I had realized a long time ago that mine had escaped me" (Sartre), and thus, the beginning doesn't exist on a timeline, but can exist at any point. Concept
Final: Nausea
