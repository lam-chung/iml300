Project 1: Interactive Audio & Visual with p5.js

I was inspired by haunted history of the Chateau Marmont (a known haven for celebrities from the age of old hollywood to now), and of the obsession/voyeuristic nature of celebrity. This interactive scene is of the hallway of the Chateau Marmont hotel, following a glint of light, until it reverses its view from inside the room.

I used an arrow and zoom transition inspired by the Google Maps streetview, which is supposed to allude to the voyeurism and the expectation of visibility from celebrities. As Google Maps streetview makes streets visibly accessible to the public, placing the arrow feature inside a confined space creates a tension between privacy and visibility that I found very interesting.

The last image is of an eye looking through the peephole from inside of the room, back out into the hallway, signifying that there was actually no one trying to look inside the room, but rather, a psychological paranoia of the inhabitant the entire time. Playing on the themes of visibility and privacy, when visibility is constantly expected of oneself, even private moments can feel like a spectacle.

Final: The Chateau

Project 2: HyperNarrative

Inspired by Martin Scorsese's surreal film After Hours (1985), in which a man in New York endures one mishap after another, entering a nightmarish series of events that prevent him from going home, I wanted to create an interactive storyline traversing through urban American Realist paintings by the artist, Edward Hopper, accompanied by quotes from Jean Paul Sartre's "Nausea" (1938). The story follows different characters in Hopper's paintings in their idle stares amidst their mundane realities, along with the existentialist, somewhat nihilistic words of Sartre. All their storylines lead to the most surreal of Hopper's works (and his last painting), "Two Comedians" (1966), with one of the most affirming and hopeful excerpt from "Nausea," bridging the dichotomies of isolation and collective conscious. Another detail to note is every time "beginning" is mentioned, it is hyperlinked to a randomized page, which correlates to Sartre's view of non-linear time; as he states, "all that was not present did not exist. The past did not exist. Not at all. Not in things, not even in my thoughts. It is true that I had realized a long time ago that mine had escaped me" (Sartre), and thus, the beginning doesn't exist on a timeline, but can exist at any point.

Project 3: Networked Care

Hot/Humid is an evolving spotlight of the designers and brands that represent the up and coming fashion scene of Vietnam. Born from a resilient history and rich culture, Vietnamese fashion's unique cross-pollination of climate, culture, and nightlife show how vibrant the country's youth culture truly is. Because of it's tropical climate (hence the name, Hot/Humid), the fashion is not only utilitarian in its ability to withstand heat and humidity, but also avant-garde, displaying its artistry through abstraction and deconstruction. As a global manufacturer, Vietnam's fashion scene is based on craftsmenship, bringing these styles straight from the source.

Final: Hot/Humid

Vibe Coding

To be honest, I'm not a fan on vibe coding full websites, especially when learning how to code; rather, I think the use of AI within coding should be for specific elements or debugging. I think using vibe coding as a tool for mockups could be useful. However, I found myself pretty dissatisfied with the results of the vibe coding exercises, as the code generated was often not functional or did not quite meet my expectations regardless of how much I refined my prompts. With the pace that AI is developing, however, I do believe it could be a very strong tool, especially if there were specific AI tools designed to not only generate code, but really develop websites.

    • Thoughts

    I went into this class excited to utilize web coding to make my portfolio website more interactive, though, I was not confident in my coding abilities having taken IML 288. However, through learning p5.js and HTML/CSS, I feel a little more capable (which is a big step for me and my relationship to coding) and am excited to start implementing these skills in my website and design practice. I also enjoyed the class crit sessions, as seeing my classmate's works were really inspiring to me. I have gained a lot through this class and am excited to continue coding in the future!
